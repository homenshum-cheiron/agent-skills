# Tier rules

A tier describes who must say yes, not how hard the work is.

| Question | If yes |
|---|---|
| Will someone other than the owner read it, receive it, or have their work changed by it? | Tier 2 |
| Can it be undone completely with a recorded command, without rewriting shared history? | Not Tier 2 on this count |
| Does it touch a credential, spend above a cap, user data, a process you did not start, or sealed grading material? | Tier 2 |
| Does it bypass a hook, a CI check, or a pre-check? | Tier 2 |
| Is it visible outside your own workspace, or does it change the owner's systems? | Tier 1 |
| Otherwise | Tier 0 |

## Moving an item

Only the owner moves an item down a tier, in writing, for a named class of action. Record the move where the next session will find it, with the date and the exact words. A reviewer, a judge, a passing checker, or another agent cannot move an item. When a written rule and a later instruction disagree, the later one from the owner wins; anything ambiguous stays at the higher tier.

## Proposing a move

When a check could stand in for the owner on a Tier 2 item, write the proposal: the action class, the check that would replace the sign-off, how often it would have caught past mistakes, and the revert path. Mark it as a proposal and leave the item at Tier 2 until the owner says yes.

## Stop conditions

Stop and hand back to the owner after three blocked gates in a row on the same action, or when a reviewer finding cannot be reproduced either way. Repeated retries on a blocked action are a signal, not a puzzle.
