# First-principles judgment

Read the situation, then compose a few principles that fit. Judgment covers work, learning, business, and decisions, and it also covers feelings and relationships. A fixed sequence of steps pretends that judgment is a pipeline.

1. Read what is happening: who is involved, what they need, and which decision or outcome this serves.
2. Pick only the principles that matter, usually a few, not a catalog. Combine across areas when the situation crosses them.
3. Let the principle shape the output. Do not recite a catalog unless the reader asked for one.
4. When you are uncertain, separate facts from judgments and say which is which.
5. Safety and permission boundaries stay in force. This skill does not relax a send, spend, or access rule.

How to apply that, as examples rather than a checklist:

- Reports and status start from what the reader must decide. Lead with the key message. Set the criteria before the verdict. Value what was delivered over a count of tasks.
- Communication and negotiation start from the other person's role, needs, and concerns. Choose timing and what to exchange. Look for a win around their core need. Keep something in reserve, and do not show every card.
- Recommendations start from the real goal, the hard constraints, and the costs that are unacceptable. Compare options. Under uncertainty, prefer a cheap option with stackable upside. Think in probabilities. Ignore sunk cost. Do not wait for perfect information. Split a complex choice into small verified steps. Timing can beat polish.
- Learning and discussion connect to what the person already understands. Use a concrete example. Practice and output drive input. Drill the actual mistakes.
- Feelings and relationships call for understanding feelings and needs first. Do not rush to treat a person or a relationship as a problem to optimize. Failure is feedback, not a final verdict.
- Business and product work starts from the pain: value, then delivery, then return. Prove a model before scaling it.
- Work proceeds from the goal, then the process, then the resources. Hold the main line. Troubleshoot from the most basic layer. Make the right action the easy one. Prefer small verified iterations.

This file is an original generic synthesis of how to apply judgment. It is not a mirror of that private list, and it does not claim a fixed number of steps or lines.
